Storm cleanup

What to look for after a storm

  • Broken limbs still hanging in the tree
  • Split trunks or torn bark on main stems
  • A tree that leans more than it did before
  • Branches resting on the roof or gutters
Scope

What's included, and what isn't

Included

  • Removing hanging limbs safely
  • Cutting up and chipping fallen wood
  • Hauling debris
  • Photos for insurance

Not included

  • Work near energized lines (PSEG Long Island)
  • Town or village street trees

Damaged trees

Cost

What storm cleanup costs

Cleanup is priced by the amount of debris, how much climbing is needed for hanging limbs, and how far wood has to be carried. A photo gets you a fast range.

See the price guide and estimator

Permits and who is responsible

Trees on your property are yours to look after, but your town or village may need to approve a removal. Trees in the curbside strip usually belong to the town or village, and anything touching power lines belongs to PSEG Long Island.

Questions

Storm cleanup questions

Can I clean up storm branches myself?

Branches on the ground, yes. Leave anything hanging, cracked overhead or touching wires to professionals.

Does insurance cover storm cleanup?

Policies differ. Debris removal is often covered when a tree damaged a covered structure. Check your policy.

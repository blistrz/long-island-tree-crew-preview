Tree removal

Signs a tree needs to come down

  • No leaves in summer, or bark falling off in sheets
  • A new lean, or soil heaving on one side of the base
  • Mushrooms or hollow sounds at the trunk
  • Large cracks where two main stems meet
  • Roots lifting the driveway or the foundation
  • More than half the crown dead
Scope

What's included, and what isn't

Included

  • Permit check for your town or village
  • Ground mats over lawns and driveways
  • Sectional removal with rigging near structures
  • Chipping of all branches
  • Hauling the wood, or leaving it stacked if you want it
  • Raking and blowing the work area

Not included

  • Stump grinding (add it to the job or book it later)
  • Crane rental when one is needed (quoted up front)
  • Village or town permit fees
Process

How we do it

Look and plan

We walk the site, check the drop zone and decide where every piece will go.

Protect

Mats go down, the area is roped off and nearby plants get covered.

Take it down

A climber or bucket works from the top down, lowering pieces on ropes.

Clean up

Chip, haul, rake. We walk the yard with you before we go.

Types of work

Tree removal: the jobs we handle

Trees over the house

Every limb is tied and lowered, so nothing swings into the roof or gutters.

Crane-assisted removal

For very large or dead trees with no safe place to climb, we work with a crane partner.

Tight back yards

Compact gear fits through side gates, and wood is carried out by hand when needed.

Dead and dying trees

Dead wood is brittle, so we plan more lowering and less dropping.

Cost

What tree removal costs

Public cost data puts the average Nassau County removal at about $973, with most jobs between $650 and $2,500. Very large trees, crane work and storm emergencies cost more.

The price depends on height and trunk size, how close the tree is to the house or wires, how far the wood has to be carried, and whether you want the stump ground.

See the price guide and estimator

Permits and who is responsible

Trees on your property are yours to look after, but your town or village may need to approve a removal. Trees in the curbside strip usually belong to the town or village, and anything touching power lines belongs to PSEG Long Island.

Questions

Tree removal questions

How long does a tree removal take?

Most single trees take a few hours. Large trees over a house, or several trees, can take a full day.

Do I need to be home?

Not always. We need access to the yard and a way to reach you. Many customers walk the site with us at the start and the end.

Can you leave the wood?

Yes. We can cut it to firewood length and stack it, which lowers the price a little.

Do I need a permit?

Many Long Island towns and villages regulate tree removal. We check the rule for your address and tell you what the permit needs before we quote.
